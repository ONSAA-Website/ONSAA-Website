## Key Takeaways

* In Marxism, “austerity” can be generally seen as a tool for the capitalist class to defend their interests at the expense of the workers during an economic crisis. Austerity presents itself as cuts to the public sector and/or the privatization of state assets in order to generate and safeguard capital for the owning class.
* Premier Ford has most recently cut OSAP under the guise of “protecting education.” This includes the maximum grant amount a student can receive from 85 to 25 per cent of their tuition, increasing the amount of funding in the form of loans that need to be paid back, from 15 to 75 per cent, and decreasing the amount of provincial aid given to universities.
* Ever since the Ford administration took control over the various Toronto school boards, they have been on a crusade of austerity to reduce their budget deficits. However, this ignores the very purpose of public education and has not measurably improved the quality of education.
* Under the Ford government, public hospitals have been systematically underfunded, which has led to a drastic and noticeable drop in quality. An example is  longer ER wait times and a major understaffing of hospitals, particularly when it comes to nurses. Additionally, the  few workers who remain often face burnout, low wages, and horrible work conditions.
* In order to fight against austerity, we require communist organizations and parties to educate the working class and fight back against the capitalists.

## What is Austerity?

If you googled “austerity”, you may come across the following definition: “a set of economic policies, usually consisting of tax increases, spending cuts, or a combination of the two, used  by governments to reduce budget deficits.” (Bondarenko, n.d.) However, this definition merely describes austerity as just an economic policy used to “reduce budget deficits”.  Consequently, it fails to adequately outline the harms of austerity on the working class on a systemic level.

## How Does Austerity Function Under Capitalism?

In reality, austerity serves as a tool for the capitalist class to defend their interests at the expense of the workers. This is because in a time of economic crisis, the capitalists themselves cannot [exploit](https://www.marxists.org/encyclopedia/terms/e/x.htm#exploitation) the working class enough to sustain themselves. Thus, the state will slash spending towards public services in order to safeguard [capital](https://www.marxists.org/glossary/terms/c/a.htm) and ultimately the overall interests of the capital class (such as education and/or healthcare), under a thin excuse of “reducing the deficit.” By cutting social services, the capitalists are able to take advantage of the weakened public sector and increase the extraction of [surplus value](https://www.marxists.org/encyclopedia/terms/s/u.htm#surplus-value) from the workers, generating profit for themselves at the expense of the working class.

## Recent Austerity in Ontario

> Since getting elected in 2018, Premier Ford has been no stranger to enacting austerity measures. Most recently, the Ford administration announced major cuts to the Ontario Student Assistance Program (OSAP). These cuts include the maximum grant amount a student can receive from 85 to 25 per cent of their tuition as well as increasing the amount of funding in the form of loans that need to be paid back, from 15 to 75 per cent. Additionally, the cuts decreased state funding to public universities, leading to future tuition spikes of 2 per cent annually for the next three years (Thayaparan, 2026). These cuts make it even harder for students,especially those who rely on public services like OSAP, to be able to afford a post-secondary education. The government’s justification for this is that these cuts will lead to an increase in program enrollments that will “drive growth” rather than being “[basket-weaving courses](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/02/18/youre-not-going-to-waste-your-time-weavers-push-back-on-fords-basket-weaving-jab/)”. These comments, however, assume that most high schoolers are going into “less economical programs” like the arts or humanities. In reality, most students go into programs like healthcare, finance/business, or engineering. Additionally, even if basket-weaving courses and programs were in such high demand, these cuts are not targeted and would still hurt the students pursuing “useful” degrees. Truthfully, these cuts only serve to discourage an educated population and further turn education into a commodity that is to be bought and sold, rather than a public good that everyone has the right to enjoy.

> Unfortunately, the austerity isn’t only at the post-secondary level. Following the Ford government's countless attacks on the various Toronto school boards, we can see how direct supervision over these school boards had on the schools. According to recent reports from the boards, drastic austerity measures from the provincial government aimed at “reducing their budget deficits”. According to the Ministry of Education, “nearly $150 million in savings has been identified through responsible financial decisions that do not impact student learning in classrooms” (Rushowy, 2026). However, this ignores the fact that school boards like the TDSB and TCDSB remain tens of millions in debt without any reported improvements for students or teachers. This means that the deficit was not reduced in any meaningful way. Finally, the fact that the Ministry is placing so much emphasis on the budgets of these school boards again points to the wider problem: education is now a commodity to the Ford administration. **If the commodity does not return a profit to the capitalist class, it has failed to serve its function.**

> Ontario's healthcare sector has also been one of the most abused sectors by the Ford administration. According to the Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives, 136 hospitals have been in deficits since 2022 (“Majority of Ontario hospitals have been operating in the red”, 2026). Rather than increasing funding towards the healthcare sector, the state has instead opted to privatize healthcare and spend less money on public healthcare. As a result, the Ontario healthcare system has been falling in quality across the board. From 2020 to 2025, patient wait times for ER assessments increased by 67 per cent and the amount of hours patients spent waiting for an assessment increased by 52 per cent (“Majority of Ontario hospitals have been operating in the red”, 2026). The systematic problem of increased wait times is also due to the large-scale nursing shortage in Ontario. According to healthcare research Doug Allan, hospitals in the other provinces have 18 percent more staff per capita than Ontario. Additionally, the province’s healthcare system is plagued by a multitude of problems faced by healthcare workers, such as burnout, low wages, and horrible work conditions. (Brophy et al., 2024)

## Failings of Austerity

Despite what the owning class and their allies try to tell the public, austerity only works to harm the working class, both in the short term and long term. They try to justify their cuts by saying how “bloated” and unprofitable the public sector is, entirely failing to understand what the purpose of said public sector is for the population. By destroying the various social safety nets that people rely on to survive, austerity measures plummet the standard of living for the average person and naturally will lead to an increase in both leftist and [fascist](https://www.marxists.org/encyclopedia/terms/f/a.htm#fascism) radicalization amongst the populace. Historically, these measures have led to a drastic increase in fascist movements in whichever countries the cuts have occurred in. Most famously, in post-WWI Germany during the [Weimar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) Republic. A time where the working class German faced extreme hyperinflation of their currency, the Reichsmark, high unemployment, and high levels of austerity. However, given a competent communist [vanguard](https://www.marxists.org/encyclopedia/terms/v/a.htm#vanguard), which can successfully educate and mobilize the masses, the capitalist crisis can be used to destroy capitalism.
